Shakti Shala · Motion Study

How much should the yantra move?

Three intensities of the same Shri Yantra — nine interlocking triangles, bindu at centre, lotus rings inside the bhupura gates. All three share one clock set to a 5.5 second breath, the pace used for coherent breathing. Watch each for fifteen seconds; the difference is cumulative, not instant.

Shared clock inhale

Intensity 01

Breath

Scale shifts by 1.2%. The bindu brightens at the top of the inhale. Nothing rotates. You feel it before you notice it — which is the point.

Intensity 02

Devotional

Breath, plus the lotus rings turning once every ninety seconds inside a fixed enclosure. The figure holds still; the light moves across it.

Recommended

Intensity 03

Overt

The triangle field rotates and lines draw themselves in sequence. Legible as animation — closer to a title sequence, and intentionally not used as the production cadence.

The soft touches

Temple, bell and incense as gold line on dark, in the Victorian devotional-print register. Each keeps its own rhythm rather than sharing one loop, so the page reads as a room rather than a slideshow.

Diya

Irregular flicker on its own seed, so it reads as fire, not a loop.

Bells

Two bells swaying on offset periods, as if air moved through the room.

Incense

Smoke drifting upward, dispersing near the top. The slowest element here.

On the geometry: nine triangles generated parametrically — five downward (Shakti), four upward (Shiva) — around the bindu, ringed by eight- and sixteen-petal lotuses inside the bhupura's four gates. The enclosure order is correct and the bhupura never rotates, because a temple does not turn. But the proportions are a faithful approximation, not a temple-grade construction: in a true Shri Yantra every triangle vertex meets its neighbours at exact triple-points, and that solution is iterative. Sheetal will know the difference. Treat this as the motion decision; let her eye confirm the geometry before it ships.
